Everyone has a profile at `/<login>`: a picture (your sign-in's, or your initials), your name, a short bio, where you are, up to 4 links, the organizations you share with the person looking, the repositories you worked in this year, a graph of the last 12 months, and your activity by month. Change your name, bio, location and links in **Edit profile** (`/settings/profile`). Once you save a name there, signing in no longer changes it.

## What counts

One square is one day. Its shade says how busy the day was against your own busy days, in four steps.

| Counts | When | For whom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A commit | it reaches a repository's main: by a merge, or with the repository's history when its main first arrives (`pando init` in an existing repo, or a first push of main) | its author, and everyone named on a `Co-authored-by:` line at the end of its message, matched by their sign-in email |
| A branch opened | its first push | the person whose sign-in pushed it |
| A review | it is posted | its author |
| A repository made | it is made | the person who made it |

Pushes, merges themselves, checks and saves of shared files do not count. Merge commits never count.

- **Agents count as you.** An agent works under your sign-in and commits with your email (`pando worktree add` sets it), so its work is yours, counted once per commit. An agent's own `Co-authored-by` line matches no one and is skipped.
- **Your email is your sign-in email.** A commit counts for you when its author or co-author email is the email you sign in with. Check what git uses with `git config user.email`.
- **Only repositories you are in.** A commit counts for a person who can open the repository when it lands.
- **A day is yours, not the server's.** Squares follow the time zone of the person looking, at today's offset for the whole year.

## Who sees what

Everyone signed in sees your picture, name, bio, location, links and join date. A viewer sees your work only in repositories they can open themselves; nothing else is counted for them, and no other repository is named. Your own profile follows the same rule: a repository you leave stops showing.

## When counts show

- **After a merge,** about half a second.
- **When a repository arrives with history,** its last 12 months are read in the background, about 10 seconds for every 1,000 commits. Older commits are not read.
- **Your own work lands live.** With your profile open, the day's square steps up and glows once.
